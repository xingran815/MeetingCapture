# M3.2 — Remove speaker echo while keeping the user's voice

Issue: https://github.com/xingran815/MeetingCapture/issues/17

Runs after M3 and M3.1, before M4. Run it with a `/goal`-style loop that keeps working until the Done-when list is met or a 👤 check is reached. Branch: `m3.2-echo`.

## Background

The mic picks up the other person's voice from the speakers, so their words end up on the mic track and get labelled `You`. Measured on a Mac with a Studio Display, whose speakers and mic share one housing:

| Measure | Result |
|---|---|
| Raw echo level | about as loud as the source (about 0 dB) |
| Delay, mic behind system | 150-180 ms with the canceller off |
| Speex reduction | about 10-20 dB in most stretches, once about 2 dB |
| Stronger suppression, or a second pass | almost no change |
| `You` words in lines that mostly repeat the other side | about a third on all 3 calls (a rough count by text and time) |

M3 fixed the startup lag and widened the tail to 500 ms (D-58). That helped, but the echo is still audible.

## Goal

Cancel as much echo as possible while keeping the user's voice. That includes when the user talks over the other person, and when they deliberately repeat the other person's words.

## Hard requirement: a repeat is not an echo

Removing lines by matching text alone is not allowed, because it would delete real repeats. An echo arrives at a fixed short delay and follows the system track. A repeat comes later and has its own level, so a method has to use that kind of evidence.

## Test set (local only, never committed)

The user records one scripted call of 5-10 minutes with the current mic and speakers:
- The other person reads invented text, and the user reads their own invented lines.
- It includes the user talking over the other person, and repeating their exact words.
- A silent stretch for the user.
- Raw mic and system tracks are saved, with no canceller applied. This needs a small capture option if `--no-aec` doesn't already give raw enough audio.
- The script and its timing are the ground truth.
- `m3-echo-off` is added as a real raw echo sample. `m3-check`, `m3-check2` and `m3-check3` were already through the canceller, so they are used only for before-and-after counts.

## Methods to compare (the researcher may add more)

| Method | Note |
|---|---|
| Speex, tuned and time-aligned | the baseline |
| WebRTC AEC3 | strong on real speakers, but a large C++ dependency |
| Local neural echo cancellers, such as DTLN-aec | on-device only |
| Canceling after the call instead of live | no real-time limit, and raw audio is kept until proven |
| A transcript check using delay and level evidence | protects labels even if some echo remains |

Combinations are allowed. Everything must run locally, and audio never leaves the Mac. Apple's voice processing stays out while recording, because it breaks other call apps. Using it after the call may be researched.

## Roles

| Role | Job |
|---|---|
| Researcher | Finds the methods, and reports evidence and licences |
| Developer | Builds a shared test harness and the methods, and reports figures |
| Reviewer | Independently checks the figures, repeat handling and privacy, without seeing the developer's conclusions |

This replaces "build in the main session" for this milestone only, at the user's request. Committed tests still come from a fresh Sonnet subagent (D-49).

## Done when

1. A results table for every method, on the same test set.
2. Echo reduction in the chosen method is reported on `m3-echo-off` and the scripted call, against the 10-20 dB baseline. **Minimum 30 dB; 35-40 dB is better, so keep going past 30 where it doesn't cost the user's voice.**
3. On the script's "other person" lines: at most 5% of the words appear under `You`.
4. On the user's lines: at least 95% of the words stay under `You`, including 100% of the repeat lines in the script.
5. The other person's lines are labelled as the other side, not `You`, and the number of speakers is right.
6. No M4A is proven from cancelled audio without keeping a way back: a failure can't lose the raw recording.
7. It runs fast enough on this Mac for a real recording, with the time reported.
8. 👤 The user listens to one new real call, confirms the echo is gone or acceptable, and confirms both voices are clearly labelled.
9. `scripts/check.sh`, CI, the verifier, and the review agents the plan calls for all pass. Unit tests use invented audio only.

## 👤 What the user does

Twice only: record the scripted test call, and do the final listen and label check in item 8.

## Privacy

- Real recordings stay out of the repo.
- Subagents may open only the scripted recording and `m3-echo-off`. Their words and audio are invented or a public video.
- `m3-check`, `m3-check2` and `m3-check3` are measured only by scripts that print counts, and their text is never sent to any LLM.

## Risks

- The targets may be out of reach for this speaker and mic pair. If so, the report says so and shows the best result, and the user decides.
- A new dependency may be needed, with its licence and size stated first.


## Known from M3's last review
Measured on invented audio; whether either happens on real hardware is unknown. Measure both first.

- **The echo can end up ahead of the reference.** With the start gate, the offset is near zero and can go negative. If the echo leads, Speex removes almost nothing: 0.5 dB at 40 ms ahead, 1.4 dB at 5 ms ahead, and 18 dB at 0 ms. It's a cliff, not a slope. Record the sign of the lag across several starts, not only its size.
- **The mic can have very short gaps.** The old lag hid buffer jitter. Now the mic FIFO runs near empty, so a late buffer can write about 21 ms of zeros into the mic track. Adding about 100 ms of silence to the mic path when the gate opens may fix both, untested.
- **Cost isn't the limit.** A 500 ms tail took about 1% of the real-time budget in release, and about 10% in debug, on an M4.
